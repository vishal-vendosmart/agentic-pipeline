## Post 1
The cost estimator stares at a scanned PDF from 2019.
It is a purchase order for a custom conveyor frame.
The line item says $1,200.
The estimator does not know if that was for one unit or a batch of ten.
The document is grainy.
The notes are handwritten in the margin.

The estimator opens a mystery Excel sheet from three years ago.
It contains a list of parts from a different project.
The parts look similar.
The descriptions are not identical.
The estimator spends an hour matching part numbers across three different formats.
One is a PDF.
One is an Excel cell.
One is a Slack DM from a project manager who left the company in 2021.
The DM says the price was a special favor from the vendor.

This is the work.
The estimator must normalize for inflation.
The $1,200 from 2019 is not the $1,200 of 2026.
The estimator calculates the delta based on a generic index.
Then they adjust for the specific material grade.
The estimate finally goes into the BoQ.

There is a version of this job where the price is in a clean database, and the bid takes an hour. That is not this job.

If you have ever spent your Tuesday night hunting for a ghost price in a dead PDF, you are not alone.

## Post 2
The cost estimator opens a OneDrive folder named Archive-Old-Projects.
There are 4,200 folders inside.
Most are named with dates and internal project codes.
The estimator needs a price for a stainless steel bracket.

The search returns zero results.
The 2021 project called it a support plate.
The 2018 project called it a mounting angle.
The estimator manually clicks through folders.
They find a vendor invoice from five years ago.
It is a scanned JPG.
The text is barely legible.

The estimator finds three different prices for the same part.
Each comes from a different vendor.
Each was for a different volume.
The estimator creates a new spreadsheet to deduplicate the entries.
They match the semantic meaning of a support plate to a mounting angle.
This takes four hours of focused work.
They check three different ERP fields to see which one is current.
None of them are.

Half the work is just remembering which folder to click in. The other half is pretending the prices inside are still true.

The most expensive tool in the shop is a senior estimator's memory.

## Post 3
The cost estimator is on a call with a vendor in Germany.
They have a BoQ with 400 lines.
The vendor quote is valid for seven days.
The clock is ticking.

The estimator looks at an ERP field.
The price for raw steel was updated in 2022.
The vendor explains the price jumped from $450 to $600.
The reason is a raw material shift.
The estimator manually updates the BoQ line by line.
They search for a historical project that used this specific steel grade.

They find a project from 2015.
The price is in a different currency.
The estimator calculates the conversion.
Then they adjust for a decade of inflation.
The vendor is still on the line.
The vendor is waiting for a confirmation to lock the price.
The estimator has to find two other similar parts to see if the jump is consistent.

No one trained for this. They were hired to estimate and ended up as a reconciliation layer between four systems that were never meant to talk.

Who in your shop knows the actual cost of a custom assembly, and what happens if they leave tomorrow?

## Post 4
The cost estimator is finalizing the bid with twelve open browser tabs.
A trail of emails spans three weeks.
The final BoQ is open on the main screen.

The estimator is doing the final normalization.
They find a European vendor invoice from 2020.
The price is in Euros.
The estimator converts it to USD.
They check for a more recent quote in a Slack thread.
They find a mention of a price hike from last month.
The estimator updates the cell.
They verify the part number matches the design lead's latest revision.

In my experience, the best estimators are just the ones who have the most patience for this specific kind of misery.
They spend their days as a human bridge.
They match semantic meanings across formats.
They dedup lists that should have been databases.
They fight with OneDrive folders.

The whole number rests on whether one offscreen cell is right. Nobody else in the company can tell.

The company calls this estimation.
It is high-stakes data cleaning.
