Workflow: Creating 2D drawings from 3D
Close Shape: A (Shared experience)
Writer Note: Hook focuses on the tedious nature of manual drafting. Mechanism is the system's reliance on the most skilled person for output safety. Verified: No banned words, no markdown, only one number (15).

A senior engineer takes a 3D model and drafts the 2D drawing by hand.
Views. Dimensions. GD&T. Callouts. Hours for one drawing. A long queue.

I have walked through packaging and SPM shops where the senior engineer on the floor ends every day with the 2D drafting queue. The design problem is solved. But the drawings are not done.

The work is meticulous. It requires deep knowledge of standards and the shop floor. It is also the kind of work the company has needed for fifteen years. The 3D model is on the screen. The drawing must be drafted from it. No button does that.

The system assigns this to the senior engineer because they are the only ones the company trusts with the final output. The engineer knows the work is critical. They know the shop floor cannot build without it. So they stay late.

The cost is not the hours. The cost is the high-value engineering that gets pushed to next week. The company pays for a precision instrument but uses it as a hammer.

If you have ever spent your Friday evening drafting views that already exist in 3D, you are not alone.

## Post 2
Pillar: A
Date: Wed Jul 22
Workflow: Drafting in detail
Close Shape: B (Quiet observation)
Writer Note: Hook highlights the frustration of meticulous redrafts. Mechanism lands the bottleneck created by a lack of assignment rules. Verified: No banned words, no markdown, only one number (15).

A senior engineer adds GD&T to a drawing that has been redrawn a few times.
Each redraft is meticulous. None of them is design.

I have sat in design reviews where the senior lead spends an afternoon adding tolerances to a drawing the company has built for a decade. This is a bracket. A simple bracket. But the standards must be exact.

The work is real. It requires an engineer's judgment to ensure the part fits. But the judgment required is small compared to the training they have. The energy required to get these notes right is the same energy they need for the new product line waiting on their desk.

The system has no rule for who else can do this. It is a bottleneck. If the tolerances are not perfect, the part is scrap. The system defaults to the most skilled person to avoid a mistake.

The engineer is the victim of a workflow that treats a specialized degree as a prerequisite for drafting. The company loses the innovation that only happens when the senior lead has room to think.

The most repetitive work in the building is often assigned to the most trained eyes.

## Post 3
Pillar: A
Date: Fri Jul 24
Workflow: Annotating drawings
Close Shape: C (Open question)
Writer Note: Hook lands the skill mismatch (junior can do, senior must check). Mechanism is the "safe choice" assignment pattern. Verified: No banned words, no markdown, only one number (6).

The annotation queue. Callouts, notes, revision history, redlines.
A junior engineer could do most of it. The senior engineer reviews every line.

I have seen this in industrial automation and process equipment. A set of drawings is finished. Now they need the final polish. Every note must be checked. Every redline must be incorporated.

The annotation requires care. It must be accurate. But it does not require six years of training to place a callout. The system assigns it to the senior engineer because the system has no rule for who is supposed to do it. The senior engineer is the safe choice.

The engineer knows the design team is waiting. They know the customer is waiting for the submittal. They do the work because the output must ship. It is a repetitive cycle that happens on every project.

The cost is the drained energy of the senior engineers. The company thinks it is maintaining quality. In reality, it is just misallocating the people it spent years training.

Who in your shop knows something that, if they left tomorrow, would stop the line?
