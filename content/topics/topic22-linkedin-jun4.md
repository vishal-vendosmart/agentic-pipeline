The prototype that worked once.

You built it. It ran. It proved the concept. Everyone celebrated.

Then you tried to build 5 more. And none of them worked.

Why? Because the prototype was hand-fitted. Tolerances eyeballed. Cables routed around obstacles, not through designed channels.

The prototype engineer knew every workaround. But those workarounds weren't in the drawing. So when another tech built unit 2, they didn't know about the "tap it gently here" or "bend the bracket slightly."

A prototype proves it can work. It doesn't prove it can be manufactured.

DFM analysis of the prototype design catches these gaps. "This hand-fitted gap needs ±0.1mm tolerance for repeatability." "This cable workaround needs a designed channel."

Prototype = possibility. Production = repeatability. Two different problems.
