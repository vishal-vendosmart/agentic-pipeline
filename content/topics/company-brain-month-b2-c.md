## POST 13
The fear of the cloud is not about technology. It is about ownership.

In industrial engineering, your proprietary logic and customer-specific designs are your only real advantages. The idea of feeding that data into a shared model is a non-starter. For most, the risk of a leak is far greater than the potential gain in efficiency.

This creates a stalemate. Companies want the power of connected intelligence, but they cannot accept the loss of sovereignty. They are told to trust the vendor, but trust is not a security strategy.

Consider a company building high-precision automation for the medical sector. Every tolerance and material choice is a trade secret. If those specifics leak to a competitor, the competitive edge vanishes overnight. They cannot upload their core IP to a public cloud just to save some time on documentation.

The only viable path is a system that runs on the company server. The model and the memory stay inside the perimeter. Nothing leaves. This turns the intelligence into a company asset rather than a rented service.

When you own the keys to the memory, the tension between security and progress disappears. You no longer have to choose between being efficient and being safe.

The difference is moving from a rented intelligence to a private estate.
