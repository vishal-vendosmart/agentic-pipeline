47 parts from 12 vendors. Each within spec. Each passed QC.

Assembled? Misalignment. Interference. Backlash. The machine that worked in CAD wouldn't power on.

Welcome to tolerance stack-up. The silent killer of custom machine projects.

Here's what happened:

Vendor A machined brackets to ±0.1mm. Perfect.
Vendor B cut frame rails to ±0.15mm. Also perfect.
Vendor C delivered electronics housings to ±0.2mm. Within their tolerance.

But add them up: 0.1 + 0.15 + 0.2 = ±0.45mm total variation. The mounting interface needed ±0.3mm. The bracket holes didn't align with the frame. The enclosure didn't seat properly.

Every vendor was "right." The assembly was wrong.

Individual part tolerance ≠ assembly tolerance.

In mass manufacturing, this is solved with statistical process control and millions of units. In custom machine building? You need one partner who owns the stack-up.

Someone who looks at your 47 parts and says "this assembly needs ±0.25mm total, so we hold brackets at ±0.05mm and frames at ±0.1mm." Someone who manages the trade-offs, not just ships to their own drawing.

That's why single-partner accountability matters. Not because they're better at machining. Because they own the assembly.
