In my many years of experience running WeFab AI, I've learned something about social media.

We engineer special purpose machines. Build prototypes. Manufacture custom assemblies in batch.

And you have never seen a single photo of our work on this feed.

Not because I don't want to share. Because I can't.

The NDAs we sign don't say "don't tag the customer." They say "don't show the work." And if I anonymize it - blur the parts, change the names, strip the context - it looks synthetic. Like I made it up.

So I stay silent on the output.

But I look at other Indian engineering services companies posting "look what we built" threads with client products front and center. And I genuinely wonder: are their contracts different from mine? Or do they just not care?

Here's what worries me: when international companies see Indian firms casually posting client work on LinkedIn, they don't think "great portfolio." They start doubting whether India can protect IP.

That is the real cost. Not missing out on likes. Missing out on trust from the customers who actually matter.

So what do I talk about instead?

I talk about the process. The 3 AM vendor email. The twelve-week delay that became sixteen. The bracket that looked perfect on CAD but didn't fit in assembly. The quality check that caught a tolerance issue at the eleventh hour.

I talk about the obsession. The fact that we don't sleep when a customer's line is down. The fact that we chase tolerances most vendors ignore. The fact that we treat your delivery date as a hard constraint, not a suggestion.

Because at the end of the day, customers don't hire us for the parts we make. They hire us for the sleep they get after handing the project to us.

And that I can talk about.
