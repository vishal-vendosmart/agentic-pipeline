## Framing Principle (locked 2026-07-13)

**The pain must be on the WORKFLOW (and the company that runs it), not on the ENGINEER.**

**Rules for hooks and bodies:**

1. The engineer wants to do meaningful work. They didn't choose the repetitive tasks — the workflow assigned them.
2. The high-touch, low-judgment engineering tasks (BOM extraction, drawing conversion, revision matching) eat the energy engineers need for the real work they actually want to do.
3. The cost the company pays: lost innovation capacity, slow quotes, attrition of the engineers who could have done the real work.
4. The cost the engineer pays: drained energy, no time for the work that made them become an engineer in the first place.
5. **Never use the words "instead of", "rather than", "should be doing", "wasting time on", or any frame that implies the engineer chose the wrong task. The workflow made the choice. The company runs the workflow.**

**Examples:**
- ❌ Bad: "You pay a senior engineer $140k to act as a human copy-paste machine." (blames the engineer's value, makes salary the issue)
- ❌ Bad: "Engineers are doing X instead of Y." (implies they chose X, makes both engineer and company look naive)
- ✅ Good: "Most engineers came here to do great work. The workflow eats their time and energy before they get a chance." (workflow is the problem, engineer is the victim)
- ✅ Good: "High-touch engineering tasks kill the energy engineers need for real design." (specific mechanism, no blame)

This applies to all 4 Week 1 posts AND every future post.
