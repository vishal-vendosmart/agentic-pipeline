The engineer who designed your automation line isn't the one building it. That's the knowledge transfer gap. And it's where custom machines die.

Here's what happens:

Design team: 6 months of CAD, simulations, FEA. They know every constraint, every design intent, every "we did this because..."

Then they hand off a drawing package. PDFs. BOMs. Tolerance notes.

Assembly team: "What's this bracket for?" "Why is this clearance 5mm and not 3?" "The cable doesn't route like the drawing shows."

The design team is already on the next project. The assembly team is reverse-engineering intent from a 50-page PDF.

Tribal knowledge: lost. Design rationale: missing. Workarounds: invented on the fly, sometimes wrong.

The gap between CAD and shop floor is measured in assumptions. And assumptions kill custom machines.

The fix? Design and build together. Not "throw it over the wall."

A partner who asks "why did you design it this way?" before they quote. Who has assembly techs that talk to design engineers. Who catches intent, not just dimensions.

That's not a vendor. That's a partner.

How do you handle design-to-build knowledge transfer?
