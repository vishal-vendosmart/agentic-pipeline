The $2M custom automation line that shipped 8 months late.

Not because the engineering team didn't know what they were doing.
Not because the design was flawed.

Because they were managing 12 vendors for 47 custom parts, and nobody owned the assembly.

Vendor #3 delayed the machined brackets by 3 weeks because they "had to rework the fixture."
Vendor #7 shipped sheet metal housings within spec, but they didn't interface with Vendor #5's electronics enclosure. Tolerance stack-up killed the first assembly attempt.
Vendor #11 went silent for 10 days during Chinese New Year. No backup plan.

The project manager spent 60% of his time on email and WeChat, not engineering.
The CTO flew to 3 factories in 2 countries. Saw clean floors and friendly managers. Still couldn't ship.

Six months after the planned delivery, they had a box of parts that individually passed QC. But the machine? It didn't power on until Month 8.

This is not a rare story. This is custom manufacturing for automation, SPMs, and robotics.

The problem isn't capability. It's coordination.
