## Locked Voice Rules (apply to all 16 posts)

### Core frame: The valuable people doing the undervalued work

The engineers and sales engineers in ETO/CTO/MTO shops are **expensive, skilled, important people**. They have years of training. They are the people the company cannot afford to lose. They are not the cost — they are the asset.

The work they do is **real**. It is meticulous. It is complex. It requires engineering judgment.

But the system keeps handing them the same five high-touch workflows — creating 2D drawings from 3D models, drafting in detail, annotating, building technical documents, extracting BOMs from legacy files. The system is using a precision instrument as a hammer. The work is real. The mismatch between the person's skill and the task is the system's fault.

**The cost the company pays:** misallocated talent, drained energy, slow output, attrition of the people it spent years training.

**The cost the engineer pays:** the work they came to do gets pushed to next week, then next month, then never.

### Point of view
- First-person founder observation. ("I have seen this", "I have sat in rooms like this", "I have watched this play out")
- The writer is a founder who has walked through real shops. Not a journalist. Not a consultant. Not a marketer.

### What the post is about
- The pain must be on the **workflow / system / company**, never on the engineer.
- The engineer is the victim. The workflow made the choice. The company runs the workflow.
- The system uses valuable people for high-touch work. The mismatch is the point.

### Banned words and frames
- "instead of", "rather than", "should be doing", "wasting time on", "spent on" (when applied to engineer-as-cost)
- "we", "our", "us" — even when the company is the actor. Use "the company", "the shop", "the team" instead.
- All product names (WeFab, AI employees, AI, any solution). The product does not exist in these posts.
- "best engineer", "top engineer", "senior engineer" as a hero noun. They are people doing work, not trophies.
- **"clerical"** — even when true, it makes the engineer the problem. Use "high-touch", "meticulous", "human-intensive", "system-assigned" instead.
- **"data entry"**, **"low-judgment"**, **"low-skill"**, **"waste"** — all banned for the same reason.
- Salary framing ("$140k engineer", "paying X to do Y") — it makes salary the issue and the engineer the problem.

### Banned structures
- Em dashes. Use hyphens.
- Markdown (`**bold**`, `_italic_`, `` `code` ``, `### headings`). LinkedIn displays it literally.
- Named personas (no Sarah, no Bill, no "the engineer at Acme Corp").
- Specific fictional moments (no "4:15 PM Tuesday", no "cold coffee", no "retirement card and cake"). The "I have seen this" framing replaces these.
- Promotional language in the first two lines.
- "Thoughts?" as a closing question.

### The 5 high-touch workflows (use as the spine of pillar A)
These are the actual tasks. Posts in pillar A name them. Posts in other pillars reference them.

Each workflow shares the same shape:
- **Important.** The output ships. The shop floor builds from it, the customer signs off, the regulatory body reviews it. It cannot be skipped.
- **Meticulous.** It requires engineering judgment, knowledge of standards, attention to detail.
- **Time-consuming.** Hours per drawing, days for complex parts, weeks for a full documentation package.
- **Repetitive across a project.** Similar parts, similar drawings, slightly different each time.
- **Low value relative to the engineer's training.** A 6-year-trained mechanical engineer applying their judgment to redraw a bracket the company has built fifty times.
- **A bottleneck if not done.** The work has to ship. The high-value work is what gets squeezed.
- **System-assigned to the most skilled person.** The system has no rule for who else could do it. The senior engineer is the safe choice. The senior engineer's time is the cost.
- **Engineers are helpless.** They know the work is important. They know it has to be done. They know the design team is waiting. They do it anyway.

1. **Creating 2D drawings from 3D models** — manually drafting views, dimensions, GD&T, callouts from a 3D model. Not a click-export. A meticulous, time-consuming drafting job that the engineer feels is inevitable low-value work in a world where high-value engineering is at crunch.
2. **Drafting in detail** — adding tolerances, GD&T, finish notes to existing drawings.
3. **Annotating drawings** — callouts, notes, revision history, redlines.
4. **Creating technical documents** — proposal packages, technical datasheets, compliance docs.
5. **Extracting BOMs from legacy files** — wiring diagrams, old PDFs, scanned drawings.
6. **Assisting sales teams with technical content** — answering RFQs, building quote packages, prepping customer decks.

These are the **meticulous, human-intensive workflows**. The system assigns them to the most skilled people on the floor. The work is real. The energy required is real. The skill match is wrong.

### Allowed specifics
- Empirical numbers that sound like real observations: "11 days", "6 hours", "30%", "47 custom parts", "12 vendors", "11 drawing conversions". These add weight.
- Role-generic language: "the engineer", "the cost estimator", "the sales lead", "the project manager", "the design lead", "the senior designer", "the proposal engineer", "the inside sales engineer".
- Founder-as-witness phrasing: "I have walked through shops like this", "I have sat in meetings like this", "I have watched this play out".
- **Name the actual task in the post body** — the reader should see their week in the post. "Drafting 2D drawings from 3D models" hits harder than "high-touch workflow."

- **Shape D — Reframing:** End by naming the thing the company calls the problem vs. the thing it actually is.
