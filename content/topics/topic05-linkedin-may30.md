The real cost of multi-vendor projects isn't on the invoice. It's in the 6 months of "not my problem."

We ran the numbers on a failed automation project. $800K in parts. $1.2M total project cost. But here's what the invoice didn't show:

Coordination overhead: 2 engineers at 60% time for 8 months = $96K in salary spent on vendor management, not engineering.

Rework loops: 3 tolerance issues, 2 interface mismatches, 1 completely wrong part. $45K in scrap and emergency re-machining.

Travel costs: 4 factory visits, 2 countries, 12 days on the road. $18K in flights, hotels, and time.

Burnout: The lead engineer quit 3 months after delivery. He'd spent a year playing email tag with vendors instead of designing. Replacement cost: 6 months of lost productivity.

Opportunity cost: The machine shipped 6 months late. The customer contract had a penalty clause. $120K.

True project cost: $1.48M. Not $1.2M. Not $800K in parts.

The "cheap" quote from 12 vendors wasn't cheap. It was just fragmented.

When you can't see the full cost, you can't fix it.
