# Post 4 — Why everyone sends a bad RFQ
Status: DRAFT v1
Target length: 1000-1400 chars
Hook: If you read the last three posts and felt a little defensive, that is fair.
Position in arc: posts 1-3 established the term and the pain, this is the empathetic turn

---

If you read the last three posts and felt a little defensive, that is fair.

Nobody wants to hear their RFQ is bad.

We all have too much on our plate. We send what we can. We send it fast. And we hope the vendor fills in the gaps.

That is not laziness.

That is a system that never showed you the better way.

---

Most RFQ templates are walls.

They add fifty fields you do not understand. You fill in the ones you know. You skip the rest. The vendor gets a form that looks complete but is not.

Writer's block is real.

Most people do not know what "spec" means in machine-shop language. They know what they want the part to do. They do not know how to say it in Ra numbers and material grades.

So they say "smooth finish" and "steel" and hope.

Hope is not a specification.

---

"Good enough" feels fine when you hit send.

It feels terrible six weeks later when parts show up wrong and you are explaining a $400 overrun to your boss.

The worst part?

Nobody tells you what you are missing. The vendor does not. They are too busy guessing. Your boss does not. They just see the delay.

You find out the hard way.

---

Here is the twist nobody expects.

A Quote Ready Package takes less work than a bad RFQ.

I mean that.

A bad RFQ spreads the work across six weeks of emails, clarifications, and rework. A QRP front-loads the thinking. You specify once. The vendor quotes once. You order once.

The work does not disappear. It moves to the right place.

---

You are not bad at this.

You were never shown what "good" looks like.

What is the worst "please quote" you have ever sent or received?
