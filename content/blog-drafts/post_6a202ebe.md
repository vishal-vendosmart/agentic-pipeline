**Opening**
I used to send RFQs that were puzzles. I would send 8 files and 32 drawings to a group of vendors. I would ignore the links between them.

**H2: The Invisible Work**
Vendors don't bill you for the time they spend organizing your mess. When a vendor spends an afternoon matching BOM line 17 to drawing sheet 3, that labor is invisible. It does not appear on the invoice. 

**H2: How "Puzzles" Inflate Your Price**
Because the work is invisible, it is absorbed. The vendor either adds a "frustration tax" to the quote price or they simply guess. 

**H2: The Danger of the "Best" Bid**
A few years ago, I sent a puzzle and got the "best" bid. The vendor didn't have the best process or the best price. They just guessed the best. In a puzzle RFQ, you aren't sourcing for quality. You are sourcing for the best guesser.

**H2: Buyer Management vs. Vendor Execution**
The goal is a clean handoff. The buyer manages the data. The vendor executes the quote. When the buyer fails to organize the package, the vendor becomes the project manager for the buyer's own RFQ.

**H2: Moving from Puzzles to Packages**
An organized package removes the clerical burden. It ensures that the vendor's focus is on manufacturing and pricing, not file management. This is the only way to get an accurate, competitive bid.
